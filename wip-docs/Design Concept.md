# Media Model Website — Design Concept

*Phase 1 deliverable. Establishes the strategic foundation the original brief lacked: who the site is for, what it exists to do, how it positions Media Model, and how it should sound. Everything in Phases 2–4 (IA, homepage wireframe, prototype) should trace back to this document.*

Scope for this engagement is **English only**. The core premise is fixed:

> **We know Armenia — its people, markets, institutions, and land realities — making us a trusted partner for complex decisions.**

---

## 1. Brand foundation (what we were handed)

Two brand documents accompanied the brief — a formal **Brand Guideline v1.0 (Feb 2025)** and a **Visual Concept Presentation** from a creative agency. Together they establish a fuller identity than the brief's copy alone, and the concept doc should build on them rather than reinvent.

**Identity essence.** Media Model™ is a **research collective** founded in 2001, one of Armenia's first specialized research and advisory firms. The organizing idea from the concept work is **"Model the Way"** — explicitly framed as *"Model is not a program or method. It's people — thinking, adapting, solving."* The differentiator is the word **collective**: not a fixed team but the right expertise assembled per challenge.

**Two service lines.** Research & Advisory (social & public opinion, market & consumer, monitoring & evaluation, policy analysis) and **LARP** (land acquisition & resettlement) — full-cycle implementation on national infrastructure programs. The second is unusual for a research house and is a genuine differentiator.

**Verbal assets already in play** (from the concept presentation — a palette to draw from, not all to use at once):

- "We know Armenia — we study it, measure it, and help it move forward."
- "We know Armenia — its warmth, its will, its way forward."
- "Model the Way"
- "Fieldwork becomes adventure" (talent/collective angle)
- "Start Your Discovery Journey" / "Discover the vision at — mediamodel.am" (CTAs)
- Approved logo descriptors: *research collective*, *research and advisory*, *research services*

**Visual system** (locked by the brand guide; deeper visual discovery is Phase 4). Primary palette: deep navy `#0f2873`, brand yellow `#fad75f`, off-white `#f4f4f4`, with a secondary range of blues for data/infographics. Typography: Helvetica Neue as the workhorse, paired with a condensed display face for headlines. Stated design language: *"geometric rhythm — modern, classic, academic,"* bridging heritage and modern context. Domain: **mediamodel.am**.

The layouts most characteristic of this visual language live in `inspiration-layouts/` — see especially `Screenshot 2026-07-10 212926.png`, `Screenshot 2026-07-10 212934.png`, and `Screenshot 2026-07-10 213031.png` (flagged for Phase 4 visual discovery). Worth stating plainly: the creative agency did strong work on the identity; the disconnect is that the original website brief failed to carry that quality through. Part of this project's job is to reunite the two.

**Tension worth naming.** The brief's own copy is sober and institutional; the agency's concept is warmer and more evocative. That gap is not a problem to resolve away — it's the raw material for a deliberate tone decision (see §5).

## 2. Target audience

Confirmed direction: **international organizations and donors are the primary audience** the site is optimized around. Others are served, not dropped.

### Primary — International development organizations & donors

The marquee portfolio is overwhelmingly international: World Bank, UNICEF, OECD, EU / Council of Europe, EBRD, ADB, Save the Children, World Vision, UNHCR, OSCE, Millennium Challenge Corporation, plus prime contractors like IPSOS and Ipsos-market subsidiaries who subcontract local fieldwork. This is who the site should win.

Who they are in practice: a research manager, evaluation lead, or field/operations coordinator at an agency or prime contractor, vetting a **local partner** for a study or a tender in Armenia. They are procurement-minded and risk-averse.

What they need to see, fast:

- **Methodological rigor** — sampling, CAPI/CATI/qual capability, multi-level quality control, ethics and standards.
- **Field capacity** — proof of nationwide reach, sample sizes, ability to prime or subcontract at scale.
- **Track record** — named clients, dated projects, comparable engagements.
- **Local access & language** — the thing they cannot get in-house: real knowledge of Armenia's people, regions, institutions, and land realities.
- **Reliability** — deadlines met, a dependable partner across a multi-phase program.

Implication: credibility and evidence must be reachable within a click or two of the homepage. This audience will not be won by mood alone.

### Secondary — Domestic private sector

Armenian banks and businesses (Ameriabank, Ardshinbank, Ameria, Admosphere, and others in the portfolio) buying market, consumer, and brand research. They value local insight, commercial relevance, and speed. They respond to a more benefit-led, brand-forward register than the primary audience.

### Secondary — Government & public institutions

Ministries and public bodies commissioning policy research, M&E, and especially **LARP** on infrastructure programs (North–South Corridor, Kajaran–Agarak). They value neutrality, trust, legal/institutional competence, and delivery at scale.

### Distinct track — Talent (the collective)

Researchers, analysts, and early-career field staff who could join the network. This is the emotional "fieldwork becomes adventure" territory and it maps to the brief's *Work With Us → For Individuals*. It is a real audience but a **different job** from winning clients; it should have its own space in the IA and not dilute the primary homepage narrative.

## 3. Site mission & objectives

**Mission (one line).** Make Media Model the obvious, trusted local partner for complex research, evaluation, and land/resettlement work in Armenia — by proving, to an international buyer who has never met us, that we combine defensible rigor with unmatched local knowledge.

**Primary action (macro-conversion).** A qualified buyer initiates contact — a project enquiry, an RFP invitation, or a request to explore working together. Everything on the homepage should ladder toward this.

**Secondary actions (micro-conversions that build trust toward the primary):**

- Explore the portfolio / proof of capability (the strongest asset we have).
- Understand the two service lines and self-identify.
- See credibility signals (years, project count, client roster, standards).
- Join the collective (talent) — routed to its own path, not competing with the client CTA.

**What the site is *not* trying to do.** It is not a thought-leadership publication (Insights stays deferred, per the brief), not a portal, and not primarily a recruitment site. Those are secondary at most.

## 4. Positioning & value proposition

**Positioning statement.**
For international organizations and serious buyers of research and advisory who need reliable evidence and on-the-ground delivery in Armenia, **Media Model is the research collective that has known Armenia since 2001** — pairing rigorous methodology with deep local knowledge to turn complexity into clarity. Unlike ad hoc field teams or generalist consultancies, Media Model brings two decades of nationwide fieldwork, a flexible network of specialists, and rare full-cycle LARP capability.

**Value pillars** (the proof stack the site is built to deliver):

1. **We know Armenia.** Local depth across people, markets, institutions, and land — the premise, and the one thing an external buyer cannot replicate internally.
2. **Rigor you can defend.** Methodology, mixed methods, multi-level quality control, alignment with international standards.
3. **A collective, not a fixed team.** The right expertise per challenge — sociologists, economists, statisticians, legal and field specialists — giving agility without sacrificing depth.
4. **From research to implementation.** Few competitors span public opinion through to full-cycle land acquisition and resettlement.
5. **Two decades of proof.** 25+ years, 150+ projects, a client roster that reads as a due-diligence shortcut.

## 5. Tone of voice

**Decision: a genuine balanced blend** — not sober-institutional alone, not the full evocative concept, but a deliberate fusion.

**How the blend is structured (the operating rule):**

- **Human and evocative at the frame** — hero, section openers, transitions, the talent track. This is where "We know Armenia — its warmth, its will, its way forward," "Model the Way," and "fieldwork becomes adventure" belong. They create distinctiveness and feeling.
- **Rigorous and precise in the substance** — service descriptions, project detail, methodology, numbers. Here the voice is exact, evidence-led, and free of adjectives. This is what reassures a procurement-minded reader.

The emotional lines earn attention; the rigorous substance converts it. Used the other way around, we'd either bore the visitor or fail the credibility test.

**Voice principles.** Grounded · Rigorous · Human · Clear · Confident without boasting.

**Do**

- Lead sections with a human or place-rooted idea, then immediately substantiate with evidence.
- Write to the reader's decision: what they get, what it de-risks.
- Use concrete specifics (sample sizes, methods, clients, dates) instead of claims of quality.
- Let the numbers and the client roster do the boasting.

**Don't**

- Wallpaper the site with taglines — the evocative lines are accents; repetition cheapens them (the brief already over-repeats "We know Armenia" and "complexity to clarity").
- Stay inward-looking. The brief's copy is heavily "we, we, we"; rebalance toward "you"/outcomes.
- Let warmth read as fluff on the pages where a buyer is evaluating competence.
- Use unverified superlatives ("the best," "leading") without evidence beside them.

**Before → after (illustrative).**

- *Before (brief):* "Since 2001, Media Model has been helping organizations model the way from complexity to clarity through research, analysis, and advisory."
- *After (blended):* "We know Armenia. Since 2001 we've helped organizations turn that knowledge into decisions — through research, evaluation, and advisory grounded in two decades of nationwide fieldwork." *(Human premise up front; proof immediately behind it; outcome-oriented.)*

## 6. Messaging hierarchy

**Primary message (everything supports this):** *We know Armenia — and that makes us a trusted partner for complex decisions.*

**Supporting messages:** rigor you can defend · a collective, not a fixed team · research through to implementation (incl. LARP) · two decades of proof.

**Per-audience emphasis** (same story, different entry angle):

- *International orgs/donors:* rigor + track record + local access; standards and reliability.
- *Private sector:* local market insight + commercial relevance + speed.
- *Government/public:* trust + neutrality + legal/institutional competence + delivery at scale (LARP).
- *Talent:* belonging + growth + "fieldwork becomes adventure."

## 7. Experience & design principles (bridge to Phases 2–4)

Not visual design (that's Phase 4) — the experience rules the IA and wireframe should honor:

- **Evidence within reach.** Proof (portfolio, numbers, clients, standards) is the conversion engine for the primary audience and must be surfaced early and be easy to browse — not buried in a flat list.
- **Clarity and structure as brand.** "Model the way" and the "geometric rhythm" design language argue for a highly ordered, legible, well-sequenced page. Structure *is* a credibility signal here.
- **Credibility before persuasion.** Years, project count, and client logos should appear early enough to license the rest of the reading.
- **Accents, not wallpaper.** The bold verbal assets appear at deliberate moments; the workhorse voice carries the substance.
- **Separate the talent path.** Give the collective/recruitment story room without letting it compete with the client CTA on the primary journey.
- **Structurally bilingual-ready.** English only for now, but IA and templates should not make an Armenian layer painful later (noted for Phase 2).
