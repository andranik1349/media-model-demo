# Redesign Proposal Deck — Plan & Content Outline

*Planning document for the Phase 4 client-facing deliverable — a **self-contained HTML deck** (`Redesign Proposal.html`), built in the site's own visual language so it feels like one artifact with the homepage prototype. This is the outline to review — not the deck. Once approved, the deck assembles from the paper trail already built across `Design Concept.md`, `Information Architecture.md`, `Visual Design Brief.md`, and the homepage prototype.*

---

## 1. What this deck is for

**Audience:** the client (Media Model). They wrote the original brief; this deck shows them we reshaped it through a deliberate, reasoned process — not on a whim.

**The one job:** prove that every change traces back to a finding, and land the high-fidelity prototype as the payoff. The client should leave thinking *"they understood our brief better than we did, and they can show their work."*

**The spine (before → why → after → proof):**
> Your brief had real strengths and real gaps → here's what we learned about who this site is for → here's the architecture that fixes the gaps, each change justified → here's what it looks like built.

**Tone:** confident and diplomatic. Credit the brief's strengths genuinely up front so the critique reads as partnership, not correction. Frame problems as *"what was holding the site back from your audience,"* never as mistakes. Human at the section frames, precise in the substance — the same blend the site itself uses.

---

## 2. Guardrails (must hold across every slide)

- **Trace everything.** No change appears without its rationale and its source (a Phase 1 finding or an original-brief problem). The decisions log (Design Concept §9 D1–D6; IA §10 D7–D16) is the backbone — the deck is essentially that log, dramatized.
- **Exclude the Partners fork (D14).** Partners stayed a standalone page — no change vs. the original brief, so there's nothing to justify to the client. Keep it out of the deck entirely (per the internal decision).
- **SEO = readiness only.** Present it as *"a structure a future SEO team can build on without restructuring"* — clean URLs, one topic per page, metadata slots. Never imply we ran keyword research or a ranking strategy.
- **Core premise is sacred.** "We know Armenia…" is retained verbatim (D6). Show it as a decision we honored, not one we touched.
- **Placeholders stay labeled.** Contact details, featured projects (rotating CMS slot), project selection — flag as illustrative/placeholder wherever they appear so the client doesn't read them as final commitments.
- **The 10-section homepage is trace-worthy.** The two service lines split into two full sections (R&A leads, LARP compact/contrasting) — this preserves D10 via *relative weight*, not equal share. Note it in the prototype section as a deliberate Phase 4 layout call.
- **Frame v0 with respect (it's a teammate's work).** The earlier `v-0-examples/` concept is the concrete "before" — used as an *instructive contrast* (what a literal reading of the brief yields), never as a target to mock. Credit its right instincts (navy/yellow, the window-onto-Armenia aperture, the "We know Armenia" hero ambition); attribute the shortfall to **stopping at the brief**, not to the designer. The client has already seen v0, so showing it reveals nothing new — it earns the contrast honestly.

## 5. Build notes (for when we make the deck)

**Format & mechanics**

- **Output:** one **self-contained HTML file** (`Redesign Proposal.html`) — all CSS/JS inline, only Google Fonts via `<link>`, no external dependencies. Same discipline as the homepage prototype so the two are siblings.
- **Discrete slides, not a scroll.** Each slide is a fixed full-viewport frame; **one slide = one frame** so it maps cleanly to a PowerPoint slide or PDF page if we convert later. No long continuous scroll.
- **Navigation:** keyboard (← / → arrows, and PgUp/PgDn or Space) **plus** a visible navigation UI — slide counter, prev/next controls, and a slide-dot or thumbnail rail so the client can jump around when presenting. Deep-link each slide via URL hash (`#slide-7`) so a specific decision can be linked directly.
- **Print/export-friendly:** a `@media print` stylesheet that lays out one slide per page (clean path to PDF via browser print, and a sane base for a later PowerPoint rebuild). Fixed 16:9 frames keep the aspect ratio consistent with pptx.
- **Motion, restrained:** per-slide entrance reveals only (fade-up on slide enter); no auto-advance, no scroll-jacking. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`. Discrete slides keep transitions simple and convert cleanly.

**Design**

- **Apply the site's own visual language** — navy/yellow/off-white, Arvo titles / Sora body, yellow load-bearing, the connector-bar motif as a recurring divider. The deck should feel like the brand and demonstrate the design, not sit in a generic slideware template.
- **Before/after is the recurring device, anchored on real v0 screenshots.** The `v-0-examples/` JPEGs are the "before" throughout — v0 hero vs. portal-zoom, v0's absent portfolio vs. Our Work, v0's "Work with us" nav vs. the client/talent split, v0's inconsistent service names vs. the canonical taxonomy. Real, client-seen screenshots beat sketches — reuse the pairings table (Section 3) so the transformation is legible at a glance.
- **Annotate v0 respectfully** — callouts point at *what a literal reading of the brief produces*, never at the designer (see Section 2 guardrail). Credit v0's right instincts where they appear.
- **Pull real assets:** v0 screenshots (`v-0-examples/`), brand SVGs (`brand-assets-svg/`), photos (`photos/`), prototype screenshots. No stock.
- **Keep placeholders labeled** on every slide where they appear.

**Skills to load, read, and use** (read each SKILL.md before building):

1. **`high-end-visual-design`** — primary craft standard. Same skill that set the prototype's quality bar (custom easing, IntersectionObserver reveals, transform/opacity-only performance, hairlines over borders); keeps the deck and prototype visually consistent.
2. **`theme-factory`** — lock navy/yellow/off-white + Arvo/Sora + the connector-bar divider into a reusable theme applied across every slide.
3. **`design-taste-frontend`** — layout/typography discipline; anti-slop guardrails so the deck doesn't drift into templated slideware.
4. **`canvas-design`** — for the bespoke before/after comparison graphics (old menu vs. new nav, buried portfolio vs. Our Work) as designed visuals embedded in the slides, rather than plain boxes.

*(pptx-specific skills — `pptx`, `pptx-author`, `ib-check-deck` — are out of scope for the HTML build; they return only if/when we convert to PowerPoint.)*

**Conversion note (later, if needed):** discrete 16:9 slides + the print stylesheet make PDF export clean (browser print-to-PDF). HTML → *editable* PowerPoint is still not automatic — it'd be one image per slide into a `.pptx` (looks right, not editable) or a rebuild. The discrete-slide structure minimizes that pain but doesn't eliminate it.

**Verification step:** since the pptx QC skill is out of scope, verify by screenshotting the built slides and reviewing for number consistency (25+/150+/30+/nationwide, dates, project counts), narrative alignment, and that placeholders are labeled — plus a keyboard-nav and print-layout check.
